I guarded the chain loop, corrected two documents that asserted the opposite, and committed it as the guarantee restored. The code review then reproduced the *same* loss through the guard's retry: `session.raw_transcript` was assigned after `_judge` returned, and `_judge` runs a second decode, so a raise there reached the handler with the decoder's words in a local variable and nothing persisted. The window between "the words exist" and "the words are safe" spanned a second decode, a guard evaluation *and* a processor chain; I had closed the third of it that the objection happened to name. The same shape appeared in the rules pass: `collapse_immediate_repeats` had produced three separate defects, and I fixed the first two by adding conjuncts about punctuation and case without ever asking whether its premise — that an adjacent exact duplicate is a stutter — was true. It is not; English reduplication is an open class, and `really really`, `bye bye` and `extension 4 4` all lost a word. **An objection names an instance. The instance is evidence about a shape, and the shape is what needs the fix.** Second surprise, smaller and more embarrassing: `manu history` began working mid-sprint and a pre-existing test that called `main(["history"])` to assert the verb *refused* started listing the operator's live transcripts and printing them to the test output. The read was visible; the unacceptable half is that the same phase ships `manu history --purge`, so a test one flag away would have destroyed the artefact §8 exists to preserve, with nothing asserting anything about it.
- **Proposal**: For GOTCHAS, two, both promoted. (1) **Verify a new regression test by breaking the code, not by reading it.** I shipped two tests for accepted dispositions that could not fail: one passed the value under test as a literal argument (`_why_no_retry(10.0, biased=True)`, when the line the fix changed computes `biased`), the other asserted `>= 0.0` on a `perf_counter` delta whose default is `0.0`. Reverting both fixes left all 458 tests green. What makes this specific rather than generic sloppiness is *when* it happens: both were written immediately after watching the bug fail, so the failure felt witnessed and the test felt verified — and **the bug failing and the test failing are different events**. The check is ninety seconds: revert the fix, run the suite, confirm red, restore. I ran it only after a reviewer told me the tests were hollow. Sixth and seventh instances of a check that could not fail in this repo, and the first two written *after* the pattern was named in a gate record — which retires "we hadn't noticed the pattern yet" as an explanation. (2) **A sentinel dispatched read-only will hand-trace, and one that says so is more trustworthy, not less.** The code review was offered `pytest` and an interpreter and had neither; it disclosed that in its first line, marked two objections as inferred inside their own text, and closed by naming what to check before accepting. Ten of twelve reproduced exactly at a REPL and two produced *worse* output than predicted. Read the tool boundary in the dispatch.
- **Improvement**: **Point instruments at the product's own functions, and prove the instrument before reporting from it.** Two of the three problems I reported from measurement this sprint were in my harness rather than the product. `record_phase3_corpus.py` printed "Microphone: system default" as a pre-flight check — but `resolve_device()` returns `None` immediately for the default device without touching PortAudio, so the check verified nothing in the configuration nobody edits, and the operator hit `ModuleNotFoundError: sounddevice` inside take 1 of 10 after reading a prompt and pressing Enter twice. Then `measure_long_audio.py` recomputed `decoded_seconds / speech` itself instead of calling `guard.evaluate`, did not clamp, and I reported ">100% coverage on all ten takes" as a finding — a number the product cannot produce, because `_by_coverage` has clamped at 1.0 and documented it since the guard shipped. `measure_g1.py` reusing `tier.percentile` was the precedent sitting next to both. Worse, I compounded the second one: I hypothesised the numerator included the 400 ms leading pad, which would have made the 82.8% genuine floor really ~66% against a 70% retry threshold — a serious claim — and *checking it refuted it* (the decoder's first segment starts at 0.000). The habit that saved it was measuring before asserting; the habit that would have prevented it was calling the product's function in the first place.
- **Constraint**: Proposed for HARNESS.md — **a test whose stated purpose is to prevent a specific regression must have been observed to fail against that regression.** Falsifiable by sampling: for any test citing an objection ID or a defect in its docstring, reverting the named fix makes that test fail. Verified this sprint by sabotage for the two that were hollow and the two that replaced them; the check is mechanical and cheap enough to run on every such test at the point it is written.
